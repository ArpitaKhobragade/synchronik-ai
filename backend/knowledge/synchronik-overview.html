Synchronik and the College ERP: general questions

What is Synchronik? What does Synchronik do?

SyNchRoniK Inc. is an Indian software company that builds automation software for educational institutions. It was established in Nagpur in 2001 to address the automation needs of educational institutions, and it serves only the education sector. Its products cover campus ERP, library automation, outcome-based education (OBE) and payroll, built for AICTE, DTE, NIRF, IQAC, NAAC, NBA and university compliance.

Source: synchronik.co.in (home page and About Us).

What products does Synchronik offer?

  • SackInfo ERP: Synchronik's flagship web, SMS and mobile-enabled campus management system with 30+ modules, built for AICTE, DTE, NAAC and NBA.
  • Softlib: library automation with RFID, OPAC, MARC cataloguing, graphical MIS and housekeeping.
  • OBE System: an outcome-based education platform for CO/PO/PSO mapping, direct and indirect attainment, NBA SAR and NAAC SSR, and curriculum gap analysis.
  • Payroll Software and Autonomous Modules (listed on the website's product menu).

Source: synchronik.co.in (home page).

When was Synchronik founded, and where is it located?

Synchronik was established in Nagpur in 2001. It has two offices: the Nagpur head office and a Nashik branch, opened in 2019 to serve Nashik and Western Maharashtra. According to the website, its first customer from 2001 is still a customer today.

Source: synchronik.co.in (About Us).

How many institutions use Synchronik software?

The website states that Synchronik serves 200+ institutions, with 9 lakh+ students, 20,000+ faculty and 30 lakh+ library books managed, mainly across Maharashtra and India. Named clients include Raisoni Group (Nagpur), Anjuman College of Engineering (Nagpur), College of Pharmacy (Nashik), government polytechnics and autonomous colleges.

Source: synchronik.co.in (home page and About Us). These figures are the company's own published numbers.

What is SackInfo ERP and what can it do?

SackInfo ERP, launched in 2010, is Synchronik's web-based, multi-module campus management platform covering admissions, fees, exams, HR and MIS in one system. The website lists these capabilities: 30+ integrated modules; RFID and biometric integration; MIS reports for all authorities; multi-level access control; compliance exports in the formats required by AICTE, DTE, NAAC, NBA, MCI and affiliating universities; an Android app and automated SMS alerts; real-time graphical dashboards for management; and workflows that administrators can configure without developer help.

Source: synchronik.co.in (home page and About Us).

Does the Synchronik ERP support biometric, RFID or face-detection attendance devices?

Yes, according to Synchronik's website: SackInfo integrates with attendance hardware including RFID, biometric devices, face-detection cameras and VTS, and hardware integration has been part of the platform since 2014. The help portal does not document the steps for connecting a specific device, so contact Synchronik support for setup.

Source: synchronik.co.in (home page and About Us).

Is there a mobile app or SMS alerts?

Yes. Synchronik's website describes an Android app for students, parents and staff, launched in 2017, plus automated SMS alerts for attendance, fees and exams. In the College ERP documented on the help portal, notifications reach users by email, SMS and the in-app bell, and administrators can edit notification templates, set SMS quiet hours and set up the SMS gateway.

Source: synchronik.co.in (home page and About Us); help portal Help Centre (Notifications).

What is the OBE System?

The OBE System is Synchronik's outcome-based education platform, launched in 2025. It supports CO/PO/PSO mapping, direct and indirect attainment, NBA SAR and NAAC SSR reporting, and curriculum gap analysis. In the College ERP's module list, OBE covers PEO, PO, PSO, CO, mapping and attainment.

Source: synchronik.co.in (home page and About Us); help portal Common Base Reference (Modules at a Glance).

What is Softlib?

Softlib is Synchronik's library automation software. The website describes it as RFID-enabled, with OPAC, MARC cataloguing, graphical MIS dashboards, committee reports and housekeeping, and says it manages 30 lakh+ books. Softlib grew to 50+ institutions across Maharashtra by 2005.

Source: synchronik.co.in (home page and About Us).

What is the College ERP described in the help portal?

The help portal (help.synchronik.co.in) documents a multi-tenant, web-based College ERP for autonomous degree-granting colleges. It comes in Engineering and Management variants that share a common base. It is built on ASP.NET Core 8 (MVC) with PostgreSQL as the database; background jobs are scheduled with Hangfire, and PDF reports are rendered with Chromium. The help portal covers getting started, process flows, how-to guides, a directory of every form and report, a glossary and an FAQ.

Source: help portal Common Base Reference (System Overview) and Help Centre.

How does the ERP work? How does a college use it day to day?

Users open the ERP in a web browser (Chrome, Edge or Firefox) at the address their IT team provides, log in, and see a dashboard plus a left sidebar grouped into modules. Each person sees only the modules and forms their role allows. Typical daily work: faculty mark attendance and enter marks; clerks handle admissions, fee collection and certificates; the Controller of Examinations runs the exam cycle (schedule, hall tickets, marks, freezing, marksheets, revaluation); the principal and management view reports. The help portal's Process Flows page describes the full journey from admission to transfer certificate and the complete exam cycle.

Source: help portal Getting Started, Help Centre and Common Base Reference.

Which modules does the College ERP have?

The sidebar shows these modules (visibility depends on the user's role): Super Admin, Institute Admin (setup of programs, branches, schemes, departments, fee categories), OBE, Student (admission, profile, registration, certificates), Establishment (employees, designations), Academic (timetable, attendance, course catalogue, faculty assignments, calendar), Fees (structures, collection, refunds, waivers), Pre-Exam (exam schedule, question paper, enrolment, hall tickets, invigilation), Post-Exam (marks entry, moderation, marksheets, revaluation, photocopy), OSM (on-screen marking), Parent portal, Notifications, Reports and My Profile. The help portal's Where to Find page also documents a Library module.

Source: help portal Common Base Reference (Modules at a Glance) and Where to Find.

Who uses the ERP? What user types and roles are there?

The system serves administrative staff (institute admins, fee clerks, student clerks, payroll clerks, the Controller of Examinations, the principal), faculty, and students and parents through self-service portals. There are seven user types: SuperAdmin (platform owner), InstituteAdmin, Student, Parent, Employee (faculty, COE, clerks), Management (read-only dashboards) and External (time-bounded access, such as external valuers). Permissions come from roles such as INST_ADMIN, PRINCIPAL, COE, MODERATOR, FEE_CLERK, STUDENT_CLERK, PAYROLL_CLERK, FACULTY, HOD, STUDENT and PARENT.

Source: help portal Common Base Reference (System Overview; User Types and Roles) and Getting Started (Who can do what?).

How is each college's data kept separate and secure?

The ERP is multi-tenant: every operational record carries the institute it belongs to, and every query is limited to the logged-in user's institute (it is never taken from a URL or form field). Only the SuperAdmin role works across institutions. Passwords are stored as BCrypt hashes, sessions use an HTTP-only cookie, an idle session locks and asks for the password again, and two-factor authentication can be turned on. Every record keeps an audit trail (who created or changed it, when, and from which IP address), and records are soft-deleted rather than permanently removed.

Source: help portal Common Base Reference (Multi-Tenancy; Login flow; Idle and lock; Audit Trail) and How-To Guides (Turn on two-factor authentication).

How do I log in for the first time?

Open Chrome, Edge or Firefox and go to the address your IT team gave you. Enter the Login ID and temporary password from the IT or admin office and click Sign In. The system asks you to change the password on first login: use at least 8 characters with a capital letter, a small letter, a number and a special character, and do not share it.

Source: help portal Getting Started (Opening the software; Your first login).

If you are stuck, search the Help Centre with one or two words, check the Glossary and FAQ, then contact your college's IT support.
